# Kempt

**Build your own computer-care tool for macOS with an AI assistant:** background checks, gentle cleanup and a menu-bar status app, fitted to your Mac and your habits.

> [!WARNING]
> **Read this before you start.**
> - **Your own AI assistant writes your tool, on your computer, from this recipe.** Nobody has tested the code it writes for you. The kit's authors have never seen it and can't vouch for it.
> - **Anything that moves, renames, archives or uninstalls files can go wrong.** That includes Downloads cleanup, cache sweeps, archiving to a drive, uninstall, PDF renaming, OCR and screenshot sorting. The recipe sends everything to the Trash with an undo list and asks first by default, but bugs happen.
> - **Back up first.** Have a current Time Machine (or other) backup before you turn on any job that touches files. If you don't have one, stick to the report-only features: they read and never change anything.
> - **Read every proposal before you approve it.** Keep the Trash un-emptied for a few weeks after you start.
> - **It runs in the background.** You install the background jobs yourself and can remove them anytime; your tool's README will say how.
> - **Shared as is, with no warranty and no support.** See `NOTICE.md` and `LICENSE`. A full build uses a meaningful share of your AI plan's usage.
>
> Written for macOS 27, November 2026. macOS changes paths and settings every year; if something doesn't match, ask your assistant to check the current behavior.

## What you end up with
- **Background checks on a schedule** (hourly to monthly): usable space, swap, memory hogs, known macOS space leaks, what grew this week, security settings, new login items, SSD and battery health, available updates.
- **Optional tidy-up jobs, always reversible:** old Downloads into dated folders, rebuildable caches, app uninstall with leftovers, scans made searchable. Anything that moves files goes to the Trash with an undo list, and by default it asks you first.
- **Optional local AI** (nothing leaves your Mac): rename and file new PDFs, search your files by meaning, read your screenshots into a searchable digest.
- **A command** (`<name> report`) and, if you want it, **a native menu-bar app** that shows status at a glance.
- **A name of your choosing.**

## Start in one step
**Easiest:** open the setup page, **https://kempt-kit.github.io/kempt/**, click through a few choices, and copy the prompt it makes. Or skip the page:

Open your AI coding agent (Claude Code, OpenAI Codex CLI, Gemini CLI, Cursor's agent, GitHub Copilot agent mode, or similar) in any folder and paste:

## What happens
1. It repeats the warning and checks for a backup.
2. **Quick setup** (recommended): your tool's name and whether you want the menu-bar app. Or **Let me choose**: about 10 questions and the full menu of 30 features, with a warning before each one that touches files.
3. It looks at your Mac read-only and tells you anything urgent.
4. It builds and tests in phases, then hands you one [YOU RUN] command that starts the background checks.

## Updates
New kit versions are listed in `CHANGELOG.md`, with what an existing tool can add. Your tool checks once a week and tells you when a new version is out (you can turn that off). Either way, to update, tell your AI: *"Update <your tool> from the latest Kempt."* It shows each change as an opt-in choice and applies only what you pick.

## What it will and won't do
- It never permanently deletes anything, and it never empties your Trash.
- It never installs background jobs by itself: it hands you the one command to run, so you decide what runs on your Mac.
- It stays out of the private data stores of Photos, iCloud and Mail, out of folders you mark as protected, and out of every AI tool's files (models, settings, history, work in progress), including the AI that builds it.
- It never moves a file that's open or was changed in the last 10 minutes.
- It never changes your security settings; it tells you about them and opens the right System Settings page.
- Everything runs locally. No account, no telemetry. The only network use is installs you approve and one weekly request to check for a newer kit version (on by default, easy to turn off).
